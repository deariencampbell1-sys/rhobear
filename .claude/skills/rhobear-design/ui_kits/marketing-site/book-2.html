LESSON 2.1

The whole company in one picture.

"Build in the Hub. Shape in Designs. Capture in Captur'd. Keep it clean with Reviews. Sell it with Blueprints. Rho rides every stop."

RHOBEAR isn't a pile of separate apps you have to learn one at a time. It's a relay — each product hands its work straight to the next one, the same way a runner passes a baton. Build something in the Hub, and it's ready to be shaped by hand in Designs. Shape it there, and it's ready to be turned into screenshots and demo video by Captur'd. And through all of it, every pull request gets checked by Reviews before it ships, and Blueprints is where the finished thing actually makes you money.

Look at the diagram below. Five numbered stops, in order. Notice Rho isn't stop number six — it's the one thing that never stands still, riding along every single stop instead of waiting its turn.

LESSON 2.2

Where you build the thing.

"Talk to any model. Watch a 24-agent crew actually work. No terminal required."

The Hub is the desktop flagship — the power of a full dev harness, packaged for people who will never open a shell. Talk to any model, run a 24-agent crew, and pull from 645 skills and 148 lessons the crew already knows. Live tool anatomy means you actually watch each agent work — a spinner per tool call, not a black box that returns an answer five minutes later.

You choose where the intelligence comes from: sign into the AI subscription you already pay for — your Claude or ChatGPT does the thinking — or pull a local model with one click. Nothing heavy comes bundled by default. The Hub itself is $50 one-time; if you want a taste first, the Workbench runs the same brain, free, in the cloud.

LESSON 2.4

The business engine, not a docs app.

"A booking comes in. The bot confirms it. You never opened your inbox."

Blueprints is NOT a whiteboard or a notes app — it's the engine a small business actually RUNS on: booking and calendar, a Telegram bot per calendar, multi-mailbox email, PayPal and Stripe money rails, business artifacts, with AI wired straight through all of it. Every other RHOBEAR product that needs a calendar or a payment calls Blueprints for it — nobody keeps a second copy.

Cloud Blueprints is free to start. The desktop version is $49 one-time and runs a local qwen3:4b model that reads your email fully offline — nothing leaves the machine.

LESSON 2.5

The last 10%, by hand.

"Your crew builds ninety percent. The last ten is where tokens bleed out, one pixel at a time."

You build ninety percent of anything with your crew, the same way you do everywhere else in RHOBEAR. But the last ten percent — nudge this button two pixels left, swap that one font, fix the color that's just slightly off — is brutally slow and expensive to get from a model. So Designs gives you an infinite canvas to finish that last ten percent by hand: drag it, swap it, fix it, without breaking any of the real HTML, CSS, and JS your crew already got right.

It's MIT open source, runs entirely in the browser, and either ships with a working local stub or takes your own AI key — RHOBEAR never touches your model bill.

LESSON 2.6

The picture-taker that costs nothing.

"Letting a frontier model take your screenshots costs a fortune. You don't feel it until the bill lands."

Captur'd does the exact same job an AI screenshot tool does — for almost nothing — because it's deterministic, not AI. Feed it a map, and a swarm of captors soaks a whole site into clean, rested screenshots in one pass: every page, every viewport, light and dark. Point it at whatever Designs just finished, and pour the results into Remotion to cut hundreds of marketing videos next.

It's open source, and the free tier is genuinely unlimited — because the engine never once calls a model.

LESSON 2.7

Fresh eyes, every single time.

"A reviewer with memory anchors on what it found last time. A fresh one reads the diff cold."

Reviews spins up a brand-new reviewer on every pull request — no chat history, no memory of last week's PR. Stale context is the enemy: a reviewer that remembers what it found before starts assuming the same pattern still holds, and misses what actually changed. Reviews reads the whole repo as a graph, with a second model grading over its shoulder, and it even includes Verifies — dead-path app-maps that catch routes nobody uses anymore. There's no free tier here; it's paid-only, because it's that valuable.

Its sibling, Frontman, is a done-for-you site chatbot that runs on a tiny local model — no server bill behind it, just a domain.

Why does Reviews throw the reviewer away after each PR instead of keeping its memory?
Context is bias. When reliability matters more than personalization, a fresh read beats a familiar one.
LESSON 2.8

Not a seventh product. The voice.

"It keeps talking while it works. Most voice assistants go quiet and make you wait."

Rho isn't a 7th product tacked onto the relay — it's the voice that rides every surface. What makes it different: it keeps talking while it works — dispatching agents behind the scenes, then narrating the result on that same stream instead of going silent and making you stare at a spinner. It can see through a camera or an upload, speaks in two voices — a free on-device one and an HD cloud one — and now sits as a floating orb on the desktop taskbar.
